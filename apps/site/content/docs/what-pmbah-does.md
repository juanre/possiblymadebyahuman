You wrote something. This records how you wrote it: the typing, the backtracking, the long pause where you got up for coffee. Then it signs that record so you can show it.

## What we do

While you write, we capture the shape of your editing and sign it with a hash chain into a short URL anyone can open. Only the shape leaves your machine; the words stay with you. You can also bind the finished text to the record, by keeping a content-blind fingerprint computed on your own machine and then thrown away, so a reader can confirm that the copy in front of them is the one you signed.

## Why

It has become normal to write your own sentences and then have to prove they are yours, because a detector or a skeptic decided they looked too tidy for a human. That is infuriating, and a lot of people need a way to show their work. So we made one.

## What a reader sees

The writing as it happened. They can watch it grow letter by letter over twenty minutes, or arrive in one four-thousand-character paste. They can recompute the signature in their own browser and confirm nothing changed after you signed.

## What it cannot do

It cannot prove a human wrote it. Nobody can. What it gives you is the shape of the process, with your name on it, and a reader can make of that what they will.

## How you would fake it

Write a program that types the way a person does, with its hesitations, typos, second thoughts, and pauses, and let it drive the record. The record is the timing, so the forgery has to run in real time: faking a twenty-minute essay costs your program twenty patient minutes, and faking a week of work takes a week. Cheating stays possible, and stays as slow as writing the thing.
